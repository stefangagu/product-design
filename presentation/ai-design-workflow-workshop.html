The problem

Where the time actually goes

  • Research gets done once, then lives in one person's headThe deck is written, presented, and never opened again
  • Ideation collapses to the first idea with a championDivergence is expensive, so it quietly gets skipped
  • Prototypes are approximations that get rebuilt from scratchEverything in the mock is a guess an engineer has to re-decide
  • Handoff is a translation layer between formats that don't talkSpecs, annotated mockups, "what did you mean by this spacing"
  • Consistency drifts the moment the design file stops being updatedAnd it always stops being updated
The shape

One pipeline, eleven skills

Research→ Ideation→ Prototyping→ Validation→ Dev handoff→ Shipped
↓ feeds back into
Design system governance — consistency and drift, checked against the real app

Every skill sits at a named point in this pipeline. Nothing is a general-purpose assistant.

Mechanics

What a "skill" actually is

  • A markdown file with instructions, loaded when it's relevantFrontmatter with a name and a description, then prose. That's it.
  • Not a prompt you retype — a process your team sharesWritten once, used by everyone, identical every time
  • Lives in the repo, reviewed like codeSomeone improves the research skill, everyone gets better research
  • Invoked by name, or picked up automaticallyType /research, or just describe the task
Shared contract — 1 of 2

Ask, don't assume

  • Every skill asks before it guessesTarget user, constraints, business goal — never silently invented
  • "idk for now" is always a valid answerNothing blocks. You're never trapped in a questionnaire.
  • If you opt out, it tells you what it assumedGrounded · Inferred · Assumed — labelled inline, in the output
  • Three skills carry confidence on every line/research, /persona, /impersonate — where a confident wrong answer costs most
Shared contract — 2 of 2

Sources, and where things land

  • Real, verifiable sources onlyA URL, a named report, a transcript ID, a line of code
  • No "studies show", no invented citationsIf it can't be sourced it's marked unverified and framed as a hypothesis
  • Every skill asks where to deliver its outputMarkdown file, shareable page, Confluence, a ticket, or just the terminal
  • Nothing lands somewhere you didn't chooseIncluding: it won't open a pull request on your behalf
Stage 1 — Research & insight

Understanding that persists

/research

Research framed around a decision, not a topic. Outputs insight → implication → recommendation, with real citable sources.

/artifact-synthesis

Transcripts, notes, surveys and tickets in; evidence-counted themes out. Three independent sources before anything is called a theme.

/persona

Structured intake, played back for confirmation, written out as a reusable document — every trait labelled grounded, inferred, or assumed.

Stage 2 — Ideation

Real alternatives, not five reworded ones

/ideation

Five to seven genuinely different directions from a brief — plus A/B copy and layout variants.

  • Every direction states the bet it's makingNot "this improves discoverability" — the falsifiable claim underneath it
  • Every direction states what would kill itA direction with no failure condition hasn't been thought through
  • Forced to vary along a real axisWho does the work, when it happens, where it lives, what it optimises for
  • At least one option should be uncomfortableThe safe five are usually the same idea in five outfits
Stage 3 — Prototyping

Two tracks, on purpose

Track A — high fidelity

DESIGNERS · IN THE REAL CODEBASE

  • Real design system, real components
  • Accurate to what will actually ship
  • Needs repo access

Track B — low fidelity

PM · PO · BA · ANYONE

  • Grayscale, deliberately unstyled
  • Illustrative, never implementation-ready
  • Needs nothing at all
These are not beginner and advanced versions of the same thing. They serve different people, at different moments, answering different questions.
Track A

/design-proto — in the real codebase

  • Reads your design system before it builds anythingComponents, tokens, patterns, conventions — then reports back what it found
  • Reuses existing components by defaultComposition before creation. Most "we need a new component" moments aren't.
  • Asks permission before inventing a componentExplains what it tried and why it didn't fit. You can always override.
  • No off-token values, everOne hardcoded hex and the accuracy claim is dead
  • Builds the unglamorous statesEmpty, loading, error, overflow — where designs actually break
Track B

/quick-proto — deliberately ugly

  • Grayscale only. No shadows, no rounded corners, no polish.Hard constraints, not preferences — it will refuse to prettify
  • UX floor, and nothing above itClear hierarchy, logical flow, obvious affordances, real states
  • Compare three versions of a flow side by sideA primary use case, not an afterthought
  • No AI-slop copy"Saved items." Not "Welcome to your saved items hub!"
The constraint is the feature. Polish it, and people start reviewing the styling instead of the flow.
Stage 4 — Validation

Catch it before real people do

/usability-audit

Heuristic, accessibility and flow review — every issue gets a severity and a concrete fix. No fix, no issue.

/impersonate

Loads a persona and reacts in character to a concept, flow, or screen — flagging which reactions the persona's traits actually support.

The audit is objective — rules, contrast ratios, tab order.
The persona is subjective — and honest about how much it's extrapolating.
Stage 5 — Governance

Audit the app, not the design file

/design-consistency

Audits the real app against itself — duplicate components, off-scale type, off-palette color, and inconsistent behaviour across flows.

/drift-check

Compares what shipped against the design it came from — classifying each difference as regression, improvement, constraint, or undocumented decision.

There's no design file in this workflow. So consistency is measured against the code that actually shipped.
Stage 6 — Handoff

Where the two tracks pay off

From Track A

/HANDOFF-PR

  • "Here's a branch, review it"
  • No spec — the code is the spec
  • Nothing to translate, so nothing lost
  • Days of spec-writing → one code review

From Track B

LOW-FI PROTOTYPE

  • "Here's a spec, please build it"
  • Every state, behaviour, exact string
  • Still necessary — and still long
  • The length is the argument
Track A isn't just more accurate mocks. It's a structural fix to the handoff bottleneck — not an improvement to a deliverable.
